What it's made of

This is information only: no prices and no shop links yet. The software is free and open source, so you can build it all yourself from this list.

Computer

Raspberry Pi 4. A Pi 3B+ also works (there is a case variant for each).

Display

Two 64×32 P2.5 HUB75 LED panels, daisy-chained to 128×32 (320 × 80 mm). Look for 1/16 scan, 1R1G1B.

Adapter board

A Seengreat-style RGB matrix adapter (also sold as XICOOLEE or WatangTech). The software is set for it, and for the Adafruit RGB Matrix Bonnet or HAT with one setting.

Power

One 5 V supply rated 6 A or better into the adapter's barrel jack (5.5 × 2.1 mm). It feeds the panels and powers the Pi.

Power with one supply only. Don't also plug a USB-C supply into the Pi: two supplies on the same 5 V rail can damage one of them.

Optional

Goal horn

A USB speaker or a USB audio adapter. Not the 3.5 mm jack: it conflicts with the panel driver.

Auto-dimming

A BH1750 light sensor breakout on the Pi's I2C pins.

Mute button

A momentary push-button between GPIO 26 and ground: tap to mute the horn, hold to skip to the next game.

SD card

A microSD card to flash the software image onto.

Putting it together

  1. Chain the two panels, plug the first into the adapter board, and fit the adapter on the Pi's header.
  2. Flash the image with the Raspberry Pi Imager and power on. The board makes its own Wi-Fi network.
  3. Join it from your phone, pick your home Wi-Fi and choose your teams.

The full instructions, wiring notes and settings are in the README on GitHub.

The case

A two-part case for the Pi and both panels: the panels hold on with their own magnetic screws, and it has a power-jack hole, a light-sensor and speaker grille, and vents. The files are free to download and print yourself from the repository.
